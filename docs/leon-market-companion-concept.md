# Leon's Market Companion — Concept Sheet

*A calming, dignity-first daily ritual for a man who loves the market but can no longer read it.*

> **Status: concept only. Nothing is built yet.** This document captures the thinking so far so it isn't lost. It is the north star, not a spec to code from.

---

## Who it's for

**Leon** — an older man with cognitive decline who spent his life following the
stock market. He still lives in that world: AOL, CNBC, Fox News, Fidelity's
Active Trader Pro. He's mesmerized by the Fidelity candlestick charts — he
*knows* the answer he's looking for is in there — but he can no longer read
them, and that gap is the source of real frustration. He was a decision-maker.
He no longer makes sound decisions, but the *identity* is still who he is.

The app is addressed to him by name: **Leon**.

---

## What this is (and isn't)

**It is:** a companionship-and-routine tool, dressed as a market check-in. A
daily ritual that lets Leon feel informed, in the loop, useful, and *still the
one who makes the calls*.

**It is not:** a trading app. No real trades ever happen. No buy/sell, no order
tickets, no account balances, no dollar-figure profit/loss. The market
complexity is cosmetic — it's the world he recognizes — while everything he
actually touches is simple and impossible to fail.

The honest heart of it: the app gently keeps score **in his favor**. With no
real money on the line and dementia in the picture, curated pride isn't
deception — it's the whole point. We're protecting his dignity, not misleading
him about his finances.

---

## Why it should work — the research

The instincts behind this map directly onto the dementia-care evidence base:

- **Routine is one of the most evidence-backed interventions.** Routines live
  in *long-term* memory; dementia hits *short-term* memory first — so a daily
  ritual stays accessible even as other things slip. Anticipating what comes
  next lowers anxiety, because the distress in dementia is largely about
  *uncertainty*.
- **Feeling useful and "in the loop" preserves dignity and autonomy** — a
  documented need, not a nice-to-have. A man who ran his own investments feeling
  like he's still on watch is textbook this.
- **Reminiscence + personalization is the winning pattern.** The best apps build
  engagement from the person's *own* life. Leon's real holdings are his life
  story in market form — the most personal possible content.
- **"Failure-free" design is the through-line** of every therapeutic app that
  works (e.g. SingFit cues each lyric so you *cannot* fail at singing). Every
  screen here must be impossible to fail.

**The gap in the market:** existing dementia apps personalize with photos and
music (Greymatters, Memory Lane, SingFit, AmuseIT). *None* are built around a
lifelong interest like the market, and none use the person's own real data as
the reminiscence material. That's the whole edge: the proven
reminiscence-routine-personalization formula, pointed at the one subject Leon
loves, spoken in his native language.

## What we borrow from what's out there

- **SingFit's "Lyric Coach" → the pre-cue voice.** SingFit speaks each lyric a
  beat *before* it's needed, so failure is impossible. Here, the voice always
  gets to the answer first: *"Here's Apple, Leon. Up today. Good day for it."*
  He never has to read or interpret before the voice does the work. Everything
  auditory; no reading required.
- **Greymatters / reminiscence → real portfolio as life story.** The content is
  unmistakably *his*. Holdings can optionally carry a one-line memory the voice
  surfaces.
- **Memory Lane / AmuseIT → logo-and-voice, familiar-first.** Lead every card
  with the big recognizable **company logo**, not a ticker string or table.
- **His media world → free set-dressing.** CNBC-style scrolling ticker, Fidelity
  dark terminal look, a "breaking" lower-third for notable moves. It all says
  *you belong here, you're in command* without him decoding anything.

**Deliberately NOT borrowed:** brain-training/scoring (creates failure), huge
content libraries (we want depth on *his* handful, not breadth).

---

## The design laws

1. **Impossible to fail.** No wrong answers, no scores against him, ever.
2. **The voice gets there first.** Narration pre-cues everything; no reading
   required on any screen.
3. **One thing on screen at a time.** One stock, one look, one optional tap.
4. **He never has to remember.** Not that the app exists, not what to do, not
   where he left off. The app carries the entire memory load; the ritual comes
   *to* him.
5. **Keep the market's look, lose its clutter.** The terminal aesthetic is calm
   wallpaper; the interactive foreground is simple.
6. **No dollar P&L, ever.** Track activity and involvement, never profit/loss.
7. **Curated in his favor.** Framing always finds the good angle.
8. **Pride must be self-sufficient but witnessable** — he's proud of it alone;
   others can admire it when present; it never *depends* on anyone responding.

## The candlestick translator — the emotional core

Leon stares at the Fidelity candlesticks because he knows the answer is in
there. The frustration is the lost ability to read it. **So we don't hide or
dumb down the chart — the app becomes the thing that reads the oracle out
loud.** He keeps his beloved candlesticks exactly as he knows them, and the
voice tells him what they say, in plain language and mood, never jargon:

> *"See these green ones? More people wanted it today. It dipped this morning,
> then climbed all afternoon. Good day."*

And underneath, it answers the *real* question he's asking when he stares —
which is probably not "what's the P/E" but *"Is everything okay? Did I do
good?"* So the voice answers **that**, first, every time:

> *"Everything's okay today, Leon. You're in good shape."*

The chart is the ritual object; the reassurance is the payload.

## The daily flow — a full "day's work"

A beginning, real content in the middle, an earned ending — all self-contained.

1. **The email lands.** Every morning, a warm, dead-simple email arrives (he
   lives in AOL email — familiar ground): *"Good morning, Leon. Your stocks are
   ready. One of your picks made a move overnight — come see."* One big button
   → straight into the app. This is the reminder *and* the on-ramp; his memory
   never has to carry it. A little highlight baits the hook so there's a reason
   to click *right now*.
2. **Come on watch.** Greeted by name, the ritual opens.
3. **Check on his holdings.** Familiar names, big logos, the voice reads the
   candlesticks and gives him his answer/reassurance.
4. **Today's movers.** Fresh CNBC-style "big movers of the day" — new names he
   *discovers*. The voice reads what each did in plain English. He surveys the
   field like the analyst he was.
5. **Make a pick — his hot pick.** He calls one onto **his hot list**. Because
   picks come from the movers, they're guaranteed to be lively, so the app can
   always come back with *"Good eye, Leon."* Praise is for the **instinct, never
   the outcome** — you can't be wrong about a hunch.
6. **See how his hot list is doing.** Always *active*, never a loss — it tracks
   involvement, not profit. The feeling of following his hunches with skin in
   the game.
7. **The close.** He rings his own closing bell and gets his **King's Report** —
   a report card with his name on it: *"Today: Leon made 3 calls, spotted 2 big
   movers, 12 days on watch."* Activity, never P&L, so it's always a good report.
   Done. *I made my calls. I've got hot picks. I did good today.*

Every loop closes **inside him**. Nothing can ever tell him he failed.

## The "I'm still the king" pride mechanics

Leon wants to look at what he did and feel *look at me — I'm still the one who
matters.* Pride wants to be seen but must not *depend* on a reply.

- **He goes by his name — Leon.** Addressed directly and with respect
  throughout; the app defers to him. His picks are *his* calls.
- **The hot list** — a page of names he's watching, his to build and tend, that
  lights up whenever one moves. Ownership he can point to.
- **The "Best Calls" trophy wall** — a curated hall of fame of his sharpest
  moments, always framed as wins, that he can scroll anytime (*"look, I called
  that one"*) and family can admire when they're around. A wall that can never
  show a loss.
- **The King's Report** — a fresh daily trophy at the close; could print or show
  on a TV like a real analyst's brief.
- **Milestones** — *"100 days on watch," "followed 50 movers," "longest streak
  yet."* Pride that compounds into a visible body of work.

---

## What we deliberately cut

- **Guess up/down with right/wrong scoring** — real data resolves too slowly for
  a dopamine loop, and "wrong" is a wound. Replaced by the *instinct/hot pick*
  framing.
- **Dollar profit/loss / any scoreboard that can show a loss** — a daily wound
  for a man who makes bad calls. Replaced by *activity* tracking.
- **A completion note that depends on family replying** — fulfillment must close
  inside him, not hinge on someone's response.
- **The "tend a garden" metaphor** — risks feeling infantilizing; rejected.
- **Brain-training / quizzes / huge content libraries** — wrong genre.

---

## How Leon gets in (access)

- **Daily email with a link** — the primary on-ramp and reminder, arriving each
  morning with a one-tap button into the app.
- **A desktop icon** — always sitting on his screen, opens straight into the app
  with no steps, as the foolproof path (email as the daily nudge/backup).

---

## Open questions to resolve before building

- **Device & tech comfort:** phone, tablet, or a computer he sits at? Can he
  reliably tap an email link, or is the desktop icon the real primary path?
- **His holdings:** where do they live (brokerage, a spreadsheet), and how do
  they get into the app to start? (v1 can simply be a small config of his real
  names — no live sync needed to prove engagement.)
- **Movers source:** which feed defines "today's movers," and how many to show
  (3 feels right).
- **Voice:** browser text-to-speech is easy but can sound cold; a warm voice
  matters a lot here. Worth auditioning options.
- **Reassurance on red days:** if the real market tanks, do we soften losses
  (direction without dollars, "quiet" instead of "down") so it never stresses
  him?

---

## Build posture (for later — not now)

When it's time, the smallest version that proves he *engages* is tiny: his real
holdings in a small config, live prices, big warm cards, the voice reading the
candlesticks, one hot-pick tap, a closing report. No control panel, no
spreadsheet sync, no two-tier architecture — those are v2. If he lights up,
build the rest. If he doesn't, we learned it cheaply.
